## A power-state change no longer stops the panel (pull request 29, issue 27)

- 0.2.7 stopped with `EXC_BREAKPOINT` in `dispatch_assert_queue_fail` when Low Power
  Mode changed (on a laptop set to use it on battery, plugging or unplugging the
  charger does this). Foundation posts `NSProcessInfoPowerStateDidChange` on a queue of
  its own; the main-actor `@objc environmentChanged` entry checked its isolation there
  and trapped. It is now `nonisolated` and moves to the main actor before
  `updateMotion()`.
- The report's frame named `statusItemClicked`: in the release build five identical
  `@objc` main-actor entries (statusItemClicked, environmentChanged, togglePanel,
  toggleFloating, toggleMenuBar) are merged into one body named after the first, and
  `environmentChanged` tail-branches into it with its own line number (602).
- No other observer is delivered off the main queue: NSWorkspace, screen-parameter and
  distributed notifications, event monitors, timers, and the config watcher all arrive
  on main.
- Checked: `scripts/check.sh`; the 0.2.7 release binary's disassembly; the fixed
  release build's thunk no longer checks its executor; a minimal Swift 6 program with
  the same shape traps before the change and runs after it; the contributor's Mac
  Studio survives toggling Low Power Mode on the fixed build, 0.2.7 does not.
- Not checked: a laptop's charger; that motion turns off in Low Power Mode.

## GPU usage is busy time weighted by clock (plan `docs/plans/2026-10-31-gpu-usage-clock-weighted.md`)

- Built by a contributor and approved by the owner on 2026-10-31 in pull request 30;
  the decision doc's amendment "GPU
  usage is busy time weighted by clock" holds what was decided and why, and
  `docs/evidence/2026-10-28-gpu-usage/` the measurements behind it. The contributor found
  the GPU figure high at rest: on an M5 Max the GPU composites two 5K displays busy
  20–50 % of the time at 338 MHz and under 1 W, and the driver's `Device Utilization %`
  showed that busy share. The figure is now busy share × weighted frequency ÷ top frequency, from the
  `GPUPH` residencies and `perf-states` table the frequency line already used
  (`GPUSample.usage(busy:mhz:maxMHz:driver:)` in `FtopCore`). When the clock is unknown
  it is the busy share alone, and when IOReport gives nothing it is the driver's figure;
  `ftop doctor` names which. `probe` prints the busy share, and `probe --json` gains
  `busy` and `usageSource`. Nothing else on the panel changed.
- After the measurements below, the contributor kept the clock-weighted definition over
  V²·f and the amendment's known gaps now carry the measured numbers.
- No new system reads: the sampler already read everything the new figure needs, so
  there is no cost table. The driver is still read each sample, for memory and as the
  last fallback.
- Checked (M5 Max, one 5K display that day):
  - `scripts/check.sh` with Xcode's toolchain; the new `GPUUsageTests` cover the
    handoff's rest and full-load numbers, the true 0, both fallbacks, and the clamp.
  - Reference images: all 98 rendered from `main` and from the branch are byte-identical.
    The sample snapshot keeps usage 0.37 (now busy 0.94 at 620 of 1578 MHz), and the
    hover is not part of the reference images.
  - `ftop probe` at rest: GPU 7–8 %, busy 34–37 %, 0.34 GHz, 0.8 W. Under
    `stress.swift`: GPU 100 %, busy 100 %, 1.62 GHz, about 84 W. `doctor` reads "busy
    time weighted by clock, from IOReport (GPUPH and perf-states)".
  - With the IOReport sampler forced to fail (a local edit, reverted): GPU 40 % from
    the driver, `usageSource` `driver`, busy unavailable, and `doctor` says so.
  - The real panel, built with `scripts/bundle.sh` and launched from `build/` with the
    GPU module on (the installed 0.2.7 quit for the run and restarted after): at rest
    the column sits far below its line (8–9 % against a line at about a fifth);
    under `stress.swift` the column fills to the top and meets the line; the menu bar's
    GPU reading followed (8–10 %, then 100 %).
  - Sizes and hover, by the contributor's hand on the real panel, recorded by
    screenshots taken whenever a hover chip was up: dragged through the plain strip
    (469×45 and narrower), the wide strip (1001×51), the vertical strip (114×356), the
    corner (443×190), compact (586×120, 598×202), full (598×346), and detailed
    (801×431, 1053×428, 1184×630). The GPU hover showed in every layout that has the GPU,
    then with the busy share (since removed, below), e.g. `GPU · 6% · busy 28% · 0.34 GHz · 0.5 W · VRAM 1.4 GB · 35°` in
    the wide strip, `GPU · 6% · busy 24% · 0.41 GHz · …` in the vertical strip,
    `GPU · 8% · busy 39% · 0.34 GHz · …` in the corner, and `GPU · 12% · busy 41% ·
    0.48 GHz · 1.4 W · VRAM 1.7 GB · 37°` in the detailed layout. The plain strip has no
    GPU, as before. Nothing was clipped in the screenshots looked at, and every captured
    window size is a layout's own size.
  - A graphics load, one case (`docs/evidence/2026-10-28-gpu-usage/game-4k120.md`): a
    game in the MuMuPlayer Android emulator at 3840×2160 and 120 fps, sampled once a
    second for 180 s, with each `GPUPH` step weighted exactly. Play, 61 s at 1.1–1.47
    GHz:

    | Busy | MHz while busy | Driver | Clock-weighted | V²·f | Power (of 84 W) |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | 58.6 % | 1171 | 49.9 % | 42.1 % (27–54) | 33.9 % | 12.0 % (10.1 W average) |

    Each figure against the power share, by segment:

    | Segment | Clock | Clock-weighted ÷ power | V²·f ÷ power |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | Baseline, 13 s | 338 MHz | 8.1× | 2.9× |
    | Loading, 79 s | about 0.9 GHz | 4.6× | 2.9× |
    | Play, 61 s | about 1.2 GHz | 3.5× | 2.8× |

    The clock did not settle: during play 36 % of the busy time was at 1620 MHz and
    about 26 % at 338–636 MHz, bouncing each frame. `ftop probe` from this branch during
    play read 45.9 % (busy 55.7 %, 1.33 GHz, 12.6 W), matching the 45 % the contributor
    saw on the panel. The driver's `Device Utilization %` read 0 six times while the
    hardware was 32–62 % busy; `GPUPH` never did. Limits: one game, one recording, one
    display; segment edges set by eye from the clock and power curves; one of the chip's
    four voltage tables; 84 W full load comes from a pure compute load.
