# Progress 2026-10-08

## Issue 6: a hidden panel came back; resizing with only the processor shown

Reported on 0.2.0 by a user with three copies of `Ftop.app` on one Mac.

- Cause of the panel coming back: a copy that is started while another runs hands over
  with a "show" and exits. That is intended (opening the app brings the panel back, the
  only way without the menu bar number or the command), but nothing said who asked.
- Changed: the handover carries the path of the copy that was started, and the running
  panel logs every show request with its source (`dev.ftop`, category `lifecycle`,
  lines starting `show:`). Opening the running copy again now also shows the panel;
  before, only opening a different copy did. `ftop doctor` lists the copies when there
  is more than one.
- Checked: the gate; `ftop doctor` on the owner's Mac (three copies listed); both log
  lines in a development run that was sent the two kinds of request. Not checked on
  screen: opening the installed app a second time from Finder or Spotlight, which needs
  the installed panel replaced.
- Not the cause: an update restart. A hidden panel has stayed hidden across an update
  since 0.1.1.
- Resizing: no defect found. With only the processor on (GPU and power hidden), drawn
  off screen at 600 dragged sizes: strip, vertical strip, columns, columns with
  numbers, largest 567 by 405 pt. A drag past that springs back, because type stops at
  1.3 times and there is nothing left to add. Whether the largest processor-only layout
  should grow further is a layout decision for the owner; nothing was changed.

## Issue 8: an M6 with three kinds of core

Reported on an M6 Mac mini (2 super, 4 performance, 6 efficiency cores): the
performance and efficiency cores were drawn as one group, and no core had a frequency.

- Cause of the grouping: the panel had two groups, the fastest type and everything
  else. The names in the hover were already right.
- Changed: a core has a group number instead of one of two kinds (`CoreSample.group`,
  `MachineShape.groups`), so a chip has as many groups as it has kinds of core; the
  owner asked for any number rather than three. Each group has its own gap and a color
  in even steps between the palette's two core colors (`PanelStyle.coreColor`); no
  color was picked by hand. A machine shape saved by an older version no longer
  decodes and is read from the hardware again at launch.
- Frequency: not fixed, cause not known. Nothing in the report says whether IOReport's
  channels, their count per type, or the power manager's tables are what differs on
  this chip, and there is no M6 here. `ftop doctor` now lists every distinct reason
  instead of the first; the reporter's `ftop doctor --states` output is what is needed.
- Checked: the gate (the fit sweep now includes 2 + 4 + 6 and 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 machines);
  reference images of both in the three palettes, light and dark, at strip, corner,
  standard, and two-column sizes, looked at; the 44 reference images of the two-group
  machine are pixel-identical to before the change. Not checked: the real panel dragged
  through sizes with more than two groups, which needs a chip that has them.
