# Progress 2026-10-08

## Issue 6: a hidden panel came back; resizing with only the processor shown

Reported on 0.2.0 by a user with three copies of `Ftop.app` on one Mac.

- Cause of the panel coming back: a copy that is started while another runs hands over
  with a "show" and exits. That is intended (opening the app brings the panel back, the
  only way without the menu bar number or the command), but nothing said who asked.
- Changed: the handover carries the path of the copy that was started, and the running
  panel logs every show request with its source (`dev.ftop`, category `lifecycle`,
  lines starting `show:`). Opening the running copy again now also shows the panel;
  before, only opening a different copy did. `ftop doctor` lists the copies when there
  is more than one.
- Checked: the gate; `ftop doctor` on the owner's Mac (three copies listed); both log
  lines in a development run that was sent the two kinds of request. Not checked on
  screen: opening the installed app a second time from Finder or Spotlight, which needs
  the installed panel replaced.
- Not the cause: an update restart. A hidden panel has stayed hidden across an update
  since 0.1.1.
- Resizing: no defect found. With only the processor on (GPU and power hidden), drawn
  off screen at 600 dragged sizes: strip, vertical strip, columns, columns with
  numbers, largest 567 by 405 pt. A drag past that springs back, because type stops at
  1.3 times and there is nothing left to add. Whether the largest processor-only layout
  should grow further is a layout decision for the owner; nothing was changed.

## Issue 8: an M6 with three kinds of core

Reported on an M6 Mac mini (2 super, 4 performance, 6 efficiency cores): the
performance and efficiency cores were drawn as one group, and no core had a frequency.

- Cause of the grouping: the panel had two groups, the fastest type and everything
  else. The names in the hover were already right.
- Changed: a core has a group number instead of one of two kinds (`CoreSample.group`,
  `MachineShape.groups`), so a chip has as many groups as it has kinds of core; the
  owner asked for any number rather than three. Each group has its own gap and a color
  in even steps between the palette's two core colors (`PanelStyle.coreColor`); no
  color was picked by hand. A machine shape saved by an older version no longer
  decodes and is read from the hardware again at launch.
- Frequency: not fixed, cause not known. Nothing in the report says whether IOReport's
  channels, their count per type, or the power manager's tables are what differs on
  this chip, and there is no M6 here. `ftop doctor` now lists every distinct reason
  instead of the first; the reporter's `ftop doctor --states` output is what is needed.
- Checked: the gate (the fit sweep now includes 2 + 4 + 6 and 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 machines);
  reference images of both in the three palettes, light and dark, at strip, corner,
  standard, and two-column sizes, looked at; the 44 reference images of the two-group
  machine are pixel-identical to before the change. Not checked: the real panel dragged
  through sizes with more than two groups, which needs a chip that has them.

## Control block: square white corners; what the menu bar shows (proposal)

- Reported by the owner with a picture: the control block had white square corners
  and a square shadow around its rounded glass. Cause: the glass was rounded with the
  layer's corner radius, which does not cut the system's blur of what is behind the
  window. Changed: the material is cut with a mask image instead, and the shadow is
  recomputed after the block changes size (`Sources/FtopUI/ControlPanel.swift`).
- Checked: the gate. Not checked on screen: the installed panel was not replaced and
  no screen capture was taken; the owner needs to look at the corners in both the
  closed and the open block.
- Proposal, not approved and not built: the menu bar shows one of processor, memory,
  GPU, network, or power, chosen by the user. Board "菜单栏显示哪一项 · 提案" on the
  design canvas, with two places for the choice: a row under "More" (recommended) or
  a row of its own in the block.

## Issue 8, second part: core channels with a cluster prefix; what `--states` prints

The reporter's `ftop doctor --states` on 0.2.2 showed all twelve core channels skipped
(`EACC_ECPU0`, `PACC0_PCPU0`, `PACC0_MCPU2`, ...) and no `table` line at all.

- Cause of "0 channels": a core channel was accepted only as letter, "CPU", digits
  from its first character. The M6 puts a cluster prefix before it.
- Changed: a core channel is the type letter, "CPU", and digits to the end of the
  name, at its start or after its last underscore (`ftop_core_channel_parse`).
  Channels are ordered by prefix, letter, then the number as a number
  (`ftop_core_channel_compare`), so `ECPU10` no longer sorts before `ECPU2`. The
  provider already took the nth channel of a kind for its nth core and never used the
  number, which is what the M6 needs: its M cores are numbered 2 to 5 after P cores 0
  and 1.
- Asked by the owner while this was done: recognise new chips from what the system
  reports instead of from names. Two lookups now fall back to that, and only when the
  known name gives nothing: the CPU tables are taken from the one device tree node
  that has `voltage-states<N>-sram` tables (not taken when two nodes have them); the
  GPU's steps from the node the graphics driver (`IOAccelerator`) is attached to. No
  table name, format, or value was added. Recorded in the blueprint.
- `ftop doctor --states` now covers the CPU and the GPU and is written on request from
  the last sample instead of on every sample: the tables used and the node they came
  from; every `voltage-states*` and `perf-state*` property in the device tree with its
  size, whether it reads as frequencies, and its first 32 bytes; all property names of
  the power manager or the GPU's node when no table was found; how the device tree
  numbers each CPU; every channel with its states and residencies, one line a channel.
  Text that does not fit its buffer (64 KB for the CPU, 32 KB for the GPU) is cut at a
  line and says so; the tables and properties come first, so only channel lines can go.
- The reasons for a missing frequency now say what is missing: no table found, no
  table with the channel's number of steps, or several that differ.
- Not known, so not done: whether the M6 has frequency tables ftop can read. If its
  tables have another name or layout, frequencies stay unavailable there and the new
  output shows the names and the bytes. That needs the reporter's output once more.
- Checked: the gate; new tests for the reporter's twelve names (kind and number), 21
  names that are not cores, and the order with and without a prefix; on the owner's M4
  Pro `ftop doctor` and `ftop doctor --states`: per-core and GPU frequency `ok`, the
  same tables as before (1 for E, 5 for P). Both fallbacks were run once here by
  giving the code a wrong node name, and the truncation by giving it a 700-byte
  buffer; both edits were reverted.
- Not checked: anything on an M6 or any chip with three kinds of core; the order of an
  M6's channels against its logical CPUs (the output now prints both); `scripts/perf.sh`
  (the per-sample text is no longer built, nothing was added to a sample).

## Issue 8, third part: a GPU with more states than its table has values

The reporter's two runs of `ftop doctor --states` on 0.2.3 (M6 Mac mini), one idle and
one with a local LLM loading the GPU:

- Device tree, node `sgx`: `perf-states` is 14 entries, the first with frequency 0 and
  13 that read as 502 to 1735 MHz; `perf-state-count` is 14.
- IOReport `GPUPH`: OFF and P1 to P15. Idle: only OFF and P1 have residency. Loaded:
  P1 and P7 to P13, 85% of the active time in P13; P14 and P15 are 0 in both runs.

- Cause of the missing frequency: ftop gave one only when the table had at least as
  many values as the channel has active states. 13 against 15 was refused outright,
  whichever states had run.
- The inference, which is not documentation: P1 to P13 are the 13 values in order
  (P13 = 1735 MHz, the top), the table's zero entry is OFF (14 = OFF + 13, which is
  what `perf-state-count` says), and P14 and P15 are listed by IOReport but not used
  by this chip. It rests on two samples of one machine: a saturated GPU sat in P13,
  the last state the table covers, and neither sample entered P14 or P15. The owner's
  M4 Pro agrees with the reading of the zero entry: `perf-state-count` 16, OFF and 15
  values, 15 states. Nothing shows what P14 and P15 are.
- Changed: the channel may list more active states than the table has values. The
  n-th state runs at the n-th value, weighted by residency as before. An interval
  with residency in a state past the table's end has no frequency and no top, with
  the reason naming the first such state ("the GPU ran in P14, which its frequency
  table of 13 values does not cover"). No frequency is worked out for a state the
  table lacks. The reason "fewer than the N states IOReport reports" is gone.
- The top of the table (`max_mhz`, the fill of the GPU's mark and the `ok` of
  `ftop doctor`) follows the same rule, so it is never shown beside a missing
  frequency for this cause, and the hover says "frequency unavailable" as for any
  other. With matching counts, or a table longer than the states, the figures are
  computed exactly as before.
- `ftop doctor --states`: the `GPUPH` line names the states past the table's end and
  says whether any was used in the sample.
- The matching moved out of the sampler into `ftop_gpu_frequency` (residencies and
  table in, frequency out, no system calls), still in `CFtopSys`, so tests reach it.
- Checked: the gate; new tests from the reporter's two samples (idle gives 502 MHz;
  loaded gives a figure above 1548 MHz, which P13's share alone guarantees), a state
  past the table with residency (no frequency, state named), matching counts, a
  longer table, no table, powered down. The nine table values between 834 and 1735
  MHz in the fixture are placeholders: the report shows only the first bytes of the
  table. On the owner's M4 Pro `ftop doctor` and `ftop doctor --states`: GPU frequency
  `ok`, 0.34 GHz while nearly idle. With the table cut in the code to 13 and then to
  1 value (reverted): the `GPUPH` line listed the states beyond, `ftop doctor` said
  `ok` for a sample that used none of them and `MISS ... the GPU ran in P2 ...` for
  one that did.
- Not checked: anything on an M6. Whether its figure is right rests on the inference
  above; a sample there that enters P14 or P15 would show as unavailable and would be
  the evidence that the inference is incomplete. `scripts/perf.sh` (the per-sample
  work is the same loop with two small arrays on the stack).
